# How a Cooperative Society is Governed

General body: all members together. It is the highest authority of the society. It approves the annual accounts, elects the board of directors, approves the budget and can amend the by-laws (amendments usually need approval of the Registrar).

Annual General Meeting (AGM): held once every year. Members should get advance notice and can ask questions about the accounts and working of the society.

Board of directors (managing committee): elected by members. It runs the society between general meetings, and elects a chairperson and vice-chairperson. The board is accountable to the general body.

Secretary or chief executive: the paid officer who looks after day-to-day work and records under the direction of the board.

Audit: every society must get its accounts audited every year by an auditor approved under the law. Members can ask to see the audit report at the general meeting.

Elections: the board is elected by members for a fixed term, as set by the law and by-laws. Under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, elections are conducted by the Cooperative Election Authority.

Registrar of Cooperative Societies: the government officer who registers societies, checks by-laws, can order inquiry or inspection, and decides disputes or appoints arbitration under the state Act. Each state has a Registrar. Multi-state societies are under the Central Registrar.

Disputes among members, the society and officers are generally not taken first to the civil court. Under most cooperative laws they are referred to the Registrar or to arbitration or a cooperative tribunal.

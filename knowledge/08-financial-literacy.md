# Financial Literacy for Farmers and Cooperative Members

Simple interest: interest is charged only on the original amount. Example: a loan of 10,000 rupees at 8 percent simple interest for one year costs 800 rupees interest.

Compound interest: interest is charged on the original amount and on interest already added. Over several years, compound interest grows the amount faster. This helps in savings and hurts in loans that are not repaid on time.

Budget: write down expected income (crop sale, dairy, other work) and expected costs (seed, fertiliser, labour, loan repayment, household needs) for the season. Keep some money aside for emergencies.

Saving: a savings account in a bank or cooperative bank, a recurring deposit or a fixed deposit are safe ways to save. Deposits in banks, including cooperative banks, are insured by DICGC up to 5 lakh rupees per depositor per bank. Credit societies that are not banks may not be covered, so ask before depositing.

Borrowing safely: prefer loans from your PACS, cooperative bank or a scheduled bank. Avoid moneylenders who charge very high interest or take your land papers or ATM card. Ask for the interest rate as a yearly percentage, all charges and the repayment schedule in writing.

Fraud safety: never share your OTP, ATM PIN, UPI PIN or password with anyone, even if the caller says they are from the bank or the society. A bank never asks for these. Be careful of offers that promise very high returns or free money. If you lose money to a fraud, call 1930 immediately and report at cybercrime.gov.in, and inform your bank.

Digital payments: use only the official UPI apps, check the name of the receiver before you pay, and keep the phone locked.

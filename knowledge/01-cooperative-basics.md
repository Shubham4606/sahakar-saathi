# Cooperative Basics and the Law

A cooperative society is a member-owned organisation. People join voluntarily to meet common economic, social or cultural needs, and the society is run democratically. The usual cooperative principles are: voluntary and open membership, democratic member control (one member, one vote), member economic participation, autonomy and independence, education and training, cooperation among cooperatives, and concern for the community.

Cooperative societies in India are mainly governed by two kinds of law. Most societies, including most PACS, are registered under the Cooperative Societies Act of their own state, because cooperatives are a state subject. Societies whose members and area of work are spread across more than one state are registered under the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, which is administered by the Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies.

The Ministry of Cooperation was created by the Government of India in July 2021 to give cooperatives a separate administrative and legal framework and to strengthen the cooperative movement.

The 97th Constitutional Amendment (2011) made the right to form cooperative societies a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(c) and added Article 43B and Part IXB on cooperative societies. In 2021 the Supreme Court held that Part IXB applies to multi-state cooperative societies, while state cooperative societies continue to be governed by their state laws.

Every society must follow its registered by-laws. By-laws are the rules of the society: who can be a member, how the board is elected, how meetings are held, how profits are shared and how disputes are handled.

# Rights and Duties of Members

A member of a cooperative society generally has these rights: to vote in the general body meeting with one vote regardless of shares held, to elect and to stand for election to the board of directors (subject to eligibility in the by-laws), to receive the annual report and audited accounts, to attend the annual general meeting, to use the services of the society on equal terms, and to raise complaints with the society and with the Registrar.

A member generally has these duties: to pay share capital and dues on time, to repay loans as agreed, to attend general meetings, to follow the by-laws and to deal honestly with the society.

Exact rights and conditions differ by state Act and by the society's own by-laws. A member should ask the secretary for a copy of the by-laws.
